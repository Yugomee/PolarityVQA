PolarityVQA: A Diagnostic Benchmark and Reliability-Aware Protocol for Multi-Object Attribute Binding in Text-to-Image Evaluation

Abstract

VQA-based evaluation is widely used to assess whether text-to-image (T2I) models satisfy compositional prompts. Existing protocols typically ask positive required probes, such as whether an object is present or whether an intended object has an intended attribute. However, positive-only verification is diagnostically incomplete for multi-object prompts: it cannot determine whether an attribute is missing, assigned to the wrong object, leaked to multiple objects, or misjudged by the VLM judge. We introduce PolarityVQA, a diagnostic evaluation suite for auditing multi-object attribute binding and VLM judge reliability. PolarityVQA augments positive probes with ownership-violation probes: affirmative-form expected-No questions that test whether a prompt-specified attribute appears on a non-owner object. It then maps probe answers into interpretable diagnostic signatures and uses reliability signals, such as absent-object controls, cross-VLM disagreement, repeatability instability, and contradiction checks, to separate automatic judgments into reliable reports, uncertain cases, and items requiring human review.

We instantiate PolarityVQA as PolarityVQA-Bench, a human-grounded benchmark with 400 prompts, 3,200 generated images, and 4,232 released probe specifications, including 800 prompt-level absent-object controls. We also provide PolarityVQA-Protocol, a label-free audit protocol for new prompt–image sets. On PolarityVQA-Bench, 22.0% of images that pass all positive required probes still contain a human-confirmed ownership violation. Reliability-aware filtering reduces VLM–human diagnostic error from 24.4% to 15.5% while retaining 73.2% of judgments as reliable automatic reports. PolarityVQA shifts T2I evaluation from aggregate scoring toward diagnostic, reliability-aware reporting.
