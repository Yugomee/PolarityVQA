PolarityVQA: A Diagnostic Benchmark and Reliability-Aware Protocol for Multi-Object Attribute Binding in Text-to-Image Evaluation

Abstract

VQA-based evaluation of text-to-image (T2I) models typically verifies required objects and attributes through positive probes. However, positive-only verification cannot reliably distinguish missing attributes, wrong-owner binding, cross-owner leakage, or VLM judge errors in multi-object prompts. We introduce PolarityVQA, a diagnostic evaluation suite for auditing multi-object attribute binding and VLM judge reliability. PolarityVQA augments positive probes with ownership-violation probes: affirmative-form expected-No questions that test whether a prompt-specified attribute appears on a non-owner object. It then maps probe answers into interpretable diagnostic signatures and routes automatic judgments into Accept, Flag, or Defer using reliability signals, including absent-object controls, cross-VLM disagreement, repeatability instability, and target-presence contradictions.

We instantiate PolarityVQA as PolarityVQA-Bench, a human-grounded benchmark with 400 prompts, 3,200 generated images, and 4,232 released probe specifications, including 800 prompt-level absent-object controls. We also provide PolarityVQA-Protocol, a label-free audit protocol for new prompt–image sets. On PolarityVQA-Bench, 22.0% of images that pass all positive required probes still contain a human-confirmed ownership violation. Reliability Routing reduces VLM–human diagnostic error from 24.4% to 15.5% at 73.2% Accept coverage.
